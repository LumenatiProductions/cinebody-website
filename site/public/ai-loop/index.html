The full AI production loop

Most “AI video” tools wait until you dump a full bin, then try to cut it in one shot. Cinebody runs the other way: brand rules shape the brief and the shoot, six jobs read every clip the moment it lands, one word clock keeps the cut, captions, and graphics lined up, and finished work is watched and turned into lessons for the next campaign.

The idea

Help while people shoot, not after the bin is full

Useful production AI should help while people are shooting and while clips are uploading, not only after everything is already in the bin.

Typical tools only help at the end (one big “AI cut”). Cinebody connects Brand Kit, on-set coaching, grading, face and camera tracking, one word clock, the paper edit, a graphics agent, and a learn-back step so each finished video makes the next shoot and edit a little smarter.

Three innovations

What actually makes the loop hold together

Brand Kit is the recipe

Voice, look, do and don’t rules, references, and graphics style live in one place. The brief, the shot list, the coach, the grader, the paper edit, captions, graphics, covers, and the end card all read that kit instead of guessing the brand from a PDF.

One word clock for the whole cut

Gemini writes a first transcript as each clip lands. When a cut is built, Scribe times every word to a few hundredths of a second. Cuts land on those words, captions read those words, graphics pin to those words. Nothing drifts clip by clip.

Finished work teaches the next loop

Finished human cuts get watched by the same model that grades footage. Their choices become examples and short lessons that feed the shot list, the paper edit, and the graphics agent. Filmers earn a reputation score from approvals and grades.

Claude Gemini ElevenLabs BlazeFace ffmpeg Remotion Imagen

Claude plans, writes, edits, and designs graphics. Gemini watches footage, grades it, transcribes it, and coaches the camera. ElevenLabs times words (Scribe), speaks interview questions, and cleans voices. BlazeFace tracks faces. ffmpeg reads camera motion and renders picture. Remotion renders graphics. Imagen paints covers. Pulse is an ops screen, not a model.

Plan
02
Idea Studio Claude

What happens: From the brand profile, Claude writes a year of calendar ideas, a swipe deck of concepts, and the next eight weeks of moments worth shooting around. You pick, it becomes a project.

03
Brief and shot list Claude, lessons from finished cuts, Imagen cover

What happens: The goal, target platforms, length, call to action, and brand rules become concrete shots, each with a prompt the filmer sees and constraints the grader checks. The shot list generator also reads short lessons distilled from finished cuts (step 16), so what worked last time shapes the next list. Imagen paints the project cover. The Recipe then tracks every shot as empty, thin, or full as clips arrive, no model needed.

Capture
04
Camera coach Gemini, one frame every 4 seconds

What happens: While recording, the app sends one frame every 4 seconds (every 7 while scouting) with the shot prompt and brand rules. Gemini returns one creative note and a working quality score, shown in the viewfinder. Underneath that, the phone itself reads every frame for brightness, focus, stability, audio level, and a smudged lens with no model at all, and drives the cue dots.

05
AI interview ElevenLabs voice, Gemini ears, Claude brain

What happens: ElevenLabs speaks the question, the person answers on camera, Gemini transcribes the answer, Claude writes the next question against the interview goal and the topics still uncovered. A silence gate stops a quiet take from producing a made-up answer. The finished question and answer map is handed to the editor (step 10) so the cut already knows the story.

06
Upload offline queue, Claude shot matching

What happens: Clips queue on the phone and survive a lost connection. Teleprompter text rides along and later helps the transcript spell names right. When someone imports footage shot outside the app, Claude looks at the thumbnails and matches each clip to a shot on the list, only asking when unsure.

What happens: The clip is fetched once and every job reads it. The grade is judged against the shot prompt, the brand rules, and the transcript, so what was said counts from the first score. If the file cannot be watched, the grader falls back to 3 to 8 stills. The face track is a real per-frame track, sampled at 6 a second and smoothed, so a moving speaker stays in the vertical crop. Camera motion is pixel math over the first 90 seconds, so the editor can prefer moving b-roll and skip shaky shots. Stuck clips get regraded every 15 minutes, and Review All redoes a whole shoot on demand.

08
The Director Claude, every 30 minutes while footage is landing

What happens: Claude reads the Recipe, the brief, who is filming, and its own past proposals, and returns at most one move: message a filmer, add a shot, say the cut is ready, or ask for more people. It runs every half hour when clips landed in the last three hours, plus one pass at end of day. Ask, auto, or quiet mode per project. The quiet rules (one open move at a time, one per project per day) live in code, not in the prompt.

09
Running editor notes Claude, rewritten when the clip set changes

What happens: Per shot, Claude keeps notes as footage arrives: the best take so far and why, the beat it serves, continuity, and what is still missing. At end of day, if the Recipe is full, it pre-drafts the paper edit and the graphics so the first time someone opens the editor the plan is already there.

Edit
10
The paper edit Claude Fable 5.1, one call, plus human examples

What happens: One call sees the whole job the way an editor would: the brief, the shot list, every clip in shot order with its score, its visual line, and its numbered sentences, the interview story map, the running notes, and four examples of finished human cuts pulled from the library. It returns the story in a sentence, the spine as ordered lines (which clip, which words, or a silent moment), a cover for each line with the reason, and what it left out. A deterministic structure check first decides whether the job is a talking head, a multi-person weave, narrated, or a montage. The machine then executes the plan exactly. It never sees the human edit of the job it is cutting.

Keystone: Cuts land on words from the one Scribe clock. Captions and graphics use those same timings, so nothing drifts clip by clip. Every pass after the plan is deterministic: the same plan builds the same cut every time, and changing the spine drags covers, captions, and graphics along with it.

12
The graphics agent Claude with web search, nine families

What happens: Before the cut exists, Claude reads the paper edit and asks what could be a good graphic here. It can search the web and draw a route, so a road trip in the story becomes a map. It designs pieces pinned to specific lines from nine families (label, lower third, intro, outro, interlude, list, popover, callout, transition) in the brand's graphics style, and can write a brand new piece as code that compiles on the server. Playbook rules ride on every call: one event at a time, hold long enough to read, never cover a speaking face. A popover can follow the person using the face track. Change any one graphic with a sentence.

13
Editor tools Claude chat, looks, transitions, ElevenLabs voice cleanup

What happens: Ask the cut a question, search clips by meaning, or tell one card what to change and only that card moves. Looks are film LUTs and an ffmpeg chain with a matching live preview. Transitions come from one list that the picker, the preview, and the render all share. Clean up the voices runs the whole voice track through one ElevenLabs audio isolation pass and normalizes loudness, then reuses it at render.

Finish
14
Render and export own ffmpeg engine, Remotion, NLE files

What happens: One neutral edit file (picture, covers, audio, captions on the word clock, graphics) goes to our own render engine. ffmpeg composites the picture, transitions, and the ducked mix. Remotion renders the graphics and captions to a transparent layer laid on top. Shotstack is only a fallback. The same edit file exports as EDL, FCPXML, Premiere, and CSV for anyone finishing in their own tool.

Deliver
15
Deliver first render is delivered, Proof of Capture roll-up

What happens: There is no approve button. The first finished render gets a public link and a delivered stamp. The delivery page plays the cut, shows Proof of Capture below it with a valid, pending, or missing tally over every source clip, and each certificate has its own verify page showing org, time, hash, and whether the signature checks out. An editor portal shares the raw footage with an auto-written throughline.

Close the loop

Learn-back from Deliver

Most of the work runs forward (shoot, understand, cut, ship). A thinner path comes back from the finished video: what the human editor kept, in what order, with what cover, how the graphics moved, and which filmers delivered. That gets written into shot list rules, the paper-edit example library, graphics rules, and filmer reputation, so the next campaign starts smarter.

How it compounds

The whole loop, in order

  1. Brand Kit fills itself from the website; Claude drafts the profile, the voice, and the filming rules
  2. Idea Studio and the brief become a shot list that already carries lessons from finished cuts
  3. On set, the coach reads a frame every few seconds and the interviewer writes the next question
  4. The moment a clip lands: certificate, transcript, grade, approve, face track, camera motion
  5. The Director proposes one move; editor notes build as clips arrive; end of day pre-drafts the cut
  6. The paper edit reads the whole job plus human examples and writes the spine
  7. Scribe times every word; cuts, captions, tighten, and graphics all read that one clock
  8. The graphics agent researches and designs pieces pinned to lines before the cut exists
  9. Our own engine renders; the first render is delivered with Proof of Capture
  10. Finished cuts get watched and distilled into lessons for the shot list, the paper edit, and graphics
Accuracy

Every step above is read off the code as of September 7. The upload transcript is Gemini; the word clock for the cut is ElevenLabs Scribe, run when a cut is built. Whisper is not used anywhere. Three things are built but not on: the camera coach is live in the phone app but switched off in the browser camera; the overnight self-training loop is written but parked behind a flag; per-organization model settings are off, everyone runs the platform defaults. Clip Doctor (upscale, stabilize) is gone. Pulse is an ops screen, not a model.
