The full AI production loop

Most “AI video” tools wait until you dump a full bin, then try to cut it in one shot. Cinebody runs the other way: brand rules shape the brief and the shoot, models score and read footage as it lands, one shared transcript keeps the cut and captions lined up, and what humans approve teaches the next campaign.

Shoot → understand → cut → ship, then a thin return path so finals improve the next brief. Brand Kit, the shared transcript, and the edit recipe sit in the middle so every step shares the same rules and the same words.
The idea

Useful production AI should help while people are shooting and while clips are uploading, not only after everything is already in the bin.

Typical tools only help at the end (one big “AI cut”). Cinebody connects Brand Kit, on-set coaching, grading, one shared transcript, the paper edit, graphics, and a learn-back step so each finished video makes the next shoot and edit a little smarter.
Typical
Shoot, dump everything, then hope an AI cut works. Nothing is scored or transcribed until the end, and each clip may get its own word timings.
Cinebody
As clips land they are graded, transcribed, and tracked. The cut builds on one shared transcript. What humans keep or reject updates the next brief and shotlist.
Three innovations

Brand Kit is the recipe

Voice, look, and do/don’t rules live in one place. Briefs, coaching, the cut, and graphics all pull from that kit instead of guessing the brand from a PDF.

One transcript for the whole cut

Scribe writes down what was said once, with timings. Edits, captions, and graphics all use that same transcript, so a cut on a word stays lined up everywhere else.

Finals teach the next loop

What humans approve or reject gets turned into clearer shotlist rules, better paper-edit patterns, graphics taste, and notes on which filmers to use again (Pulse).

Keystone
One shared transcript
Scribe turns the spoken audio into one transcript with word timings for the whole project. When you cut (yellow markers), you cut on those words. Captions and graphics use the same list, instead of each clip getting its own Whisper-style guess that drifts.
Close the loop
Learn-back from Deliver
Most of the work runs forward (shoot → understand → cut → ship). A thinner path comes back from the finished video: style, pacing, brand rules, and which filmers performed well get written into shotlist rules, the paper-edit library, graphics taste, and Pulse, so the next campaign starts smarter.
